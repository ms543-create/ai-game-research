# Art and interface

The game should feel like one coherent product even when different tools create its assets. We will establish a small art direction before generating a large collection.

## Visual brief

For the current sports concept, explore an original arena atmosphere, readable scoreboard typography, strong feedback for finding a word, and a clear distinction between selected, solved, and hinted letters. Preserve the ability to play with a keyboard and on a small screen.

## Candidate methods

- Code-native SVG, CSS, and canvas for scalable interface elements and court graphics.
- Generated concept images to explore composition and mood.
- Generated textures or sprites after a style direction is chosen.
- Manual cleanup to correct edges, simplify detail, and maintain consistency.

## Evaluation

Test the assets at their actual display size. Check contrast, text legibility, animation distraction, loading behavior, and whether visual feedback is understandable without relying only on color. Keep essential text as real interface text wherever practical.

Compare at least two states: a calm menu and a busy in-game moment. A background that looks impressive alone may compete with the clues or reduce grid readability.

Record every generated asset’s prompt, tool/version, reference inputs, edits, and terms using the [asset record](./sources.md#asset-provenance-record).
